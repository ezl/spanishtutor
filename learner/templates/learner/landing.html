Component skills, novice to mastery

Spanish isn't one skill you either have or don't. It's hundreds of small ones: past tense for finished events, past tense for describing, knowing which one a sentence needs. Every one gets tracked on its own, and separately for reading it and writing it. That is why a lesson can be short: one component skill is about one lesson's worth of work.

Brings back what's slipping

Everything you learn starts fading the moment you stop using it. Luz uses spaced repetition, one of the most consistently replicated findings in memory research, to bring a skill back into a lesson at the point you were about to lose it. Not on a fixed schedule, and not because you picked it off a list.

Lessons built from your life

The wedding you went to. The gym. Your job, your city, the trip you're planning. Grammar gets taught through the things you actually talk about, so the sentences you practice are sentences you'd really say. Not a textbook written for everyone and no one.
